# Challenge 10 — Sides and Triangles

## Mathematical focus

The previous challenge established that the interior-angle sum grows by 180° each time the number of sides increases by one.

This challenge asks the learner to look inside the polygons and find the structural reason.

From one vertex:

- 3 sides → 1 triangle
- 4 sides → 2 triangles
- 5 sides → 3 triangles
- 6 sides → 4 triangles
- 7 sides → 5 triangles
- 8 sides → 6 triangles

The relationship is:

`number of triangles = number of sides − 2`

## Main question

"An octagon has 8 sides. If you draw diagonals from one corner to divide it into triangles, how many triangles do you get?"

Expected answer: `6`.

## Follow-up

"Now connect the two ideas: if every triangle contributes 180°, can you describe the angle sum using the number of sides?"

This is intended to lead naturally to:

`number of triangles = n − 2`

then:

`sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°`

The learner has not been introduced to `n` earlier in the series. Here, `n` can emerge as a compact way to describe an arbitrary number of sides.

## Visual design

The six polygons from triangle through octagon are shown on a clean white background.

The internal triangulation uses the established Lab palette:

- orange
- green
- blue
- purple

The internal diagonals are thin and dotted so that the decomposition is visible without competing with the polygon outline.

No angle-sum values are displayed in the visual. This avoids simply repeating Challenge 09 and shifts attention from the numerical pattern to its geometric structure.

## Representation decision

The shared Number Line, Graph, Equation and Algebra Tiles are disabled for this challenge.

This is deliberate: the challenge's main work happens in the geometric construction itself. The formal algebraic representation can emerge in the follow-up rather than being presented as another panel.
